Smooth Airfoil

Developing a polishing and surface characterization procedure to produce a hydrodynamically smooth airfoil for high Reynolds number aerodynamic research.

Researching modular 3D printed airfoils that can be rapidly prototyped to cut cost and turn around time.

Summary

There has been a discrepancy in the roughness of the airfoil depending on which lens is used to measure the surface roughness. We are trying to find out what led to this discrepnacy. I am recreating the smooth airfoil by polsihing an aluminum test piece. I am also researching how we can effectively poslish 3d printed materials to the degree of polish that we can achieve with aluminum.

I am also researching how we can create a modular airfoil that can be 3D printed and assembled to cut down on cost and turn around time. First, we tested the limits of the resin 3D printer that we have in the lab. For the pressure taps on the surface of the airfoil, we needed to create channel within the modualr airfoil so that we do not have to manually insert 32 pressure taps on each prototype. We found that the smallest diameter hole for a whole channel that we can reliably print is 0.5 mm. However, we found that we can print to 0.1mm diameter hole if we print a 0.1mm hole at the surface of the airfoil, which then then the rest of the channel can exapnd out to 0.5mm.

  • One thing that I would do differently is to optimize the amount of materails printed. The test print was a half cylinder so that we could test different angles at which the printer would print. However, there were a lot of wasted material that could have been optimized.
